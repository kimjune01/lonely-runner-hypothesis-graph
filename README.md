# Hypothesis Graph

[![License: GPL v3 or later](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-GPL_v3_or_later-blue.svg)](LICENSE)

A replayable research ledger for hard problems. Each node binds a hypothesis to the exact trial that can kill it; each failure is retained and points to the next hypothesis it generated.

This repository publishes one extended case study: an inquiry into the Lonely Runner Conjecture. The mathematical problem is the test environment, not the contribution. **The repository does not claim a proof of the general conjecture.**

## The data structure

A node records:

```text
ID and hypothesis
Reasoning mode: abduction | deduction | induction
Kill condition
Exact trial
Observed outcome
Verdict: open | witnessed | killed
Credence / verification grade
Successor edge generated by the outcome
```

The governing contract is the **replay invariant**: a stranger who does not trust the author can reconstruct a node's mechanical conclusion from its recorded trial. Killed branches stay in the graph because their exact failures constrain what a successor is allowed to claim.

## Why use a hard open problem?

Hard problems expose the failure mode this structure is meant to address. A polished final argument hides false starts, while a conversational transcript preserves them without making their logical status clear. The graph keeps both the search and its audit trail: which ideas remain live, which are dead, why they died, and what their deaths taught the next attempt.

Lonely Runner is especially useful because it combines adversarial combinatorics, exact finite experiments, published bounded cases, and a still-open general case. That produces genuine uncertainty while leaving many individual claims mechanically testable.

